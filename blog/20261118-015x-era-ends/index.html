Blog · 2026-11-18

The 0.1.5.x era ends

At the end of 2026, at the beginning of 2027, while every line of work is still in motion and while the features stand ready —
0.1.6 is coming.

The 0.1.5.x line stops at 0.1.5.1. The milestones queued behind it — 0.1.5.2 through 0.1.5.5 — fold into 0.1.6, and I am aiming at the start of 2027 to hand that release out.

This is a plan, not a shipped release. 0.1.5.1 is still the version you can install, and the date below is a target. Nothing here is downloadable today.

The line stops at 0.1.5.1

0.1.5.1 shipped on 2026-11-07 and closed the 0.1.5 milestone. The plan at the time was a run of small versions after it: 0.1.5.2 for capability domains in depth and the graph type, 0.1.5.3 for the LSPE counter-asker, 0.1.5.4 for the ledger invariants and the self-audit, 0.1.5.5 for automatic parallelization.

That plan has changed. None of those four versions will be tagged. The queue stays; only the version numbers retire, and the work moves into 0.1.6.

0.1.5.2 never left the development tree. The Playground's Beta channel has been running its snapshot since the start of the month, and that channel will keep following main. The stable channel stays on 0.1.5.1 until 0.1.6 ships.

Why fold instead of shipping four more

The 0.1.5.x numbers were drawn when the work looked separable: a domains release, an audit release, a ledger release, a parallelism release. It stopped being separable. The graph type, the class system, the LSPE framework, function values and the borrow-lifetime checker all meet in the same object model and the same checker. Splitting them now would freeze artifacts at seams that do not exist, and ask you to install versions that exist to keep a queue tidy.

There is a cost, and it is not small. 0.1.6 is a bigger release than any single 0.1.5.x version would have been, and a bigger release is harder to verify. The gate has more surface to cover, the seed is regenerated more often, and the list of things that must work together is longer. I am accepting that cost because four small releases would have paid it four times without making the result easier to trust.

0.1.6 will not contain every item from four milestone lists. It will contain what the merged queue can carry and what the gate can prove. Anything that does not fit gets said out loud, in a post like this one, before the release; it will not be quietly dropped from a list.

What 0.1.6 is

The goal written down in #922 is narrow: the interpreter becomes a target. Today choose vm and no_vm emit the same bytes, so the setting is a declaration; it does not change what the artifact is. 0.1.6 makes it a choice. An artifact can be handed to an executor that adopts it on demand and judges it before adopting.

Folded into the same release is the work the 0.1.5.x line was carrying: capability domains in depth — revocation with semantics, the subject a guard does not check, information flow, a rule-by-rule conformance suite, and the descriptor as a pinned contract; digraph<K> as a compile-time graph value with a capability lattice, layout specialization and static verification; the LSPE framework's registry, scheduler, two SDK tiers, ledger invariants and self-audit; the class ladder from single inheritance through vtables, virtual dispatch and super; and the borrow-lifetime checker that ties domains and lifetimes together.

Some of this is already on main and unreleased: the LSPE framework, the first three class rungs, function values and the effects checker. I am describing where the line lands; much of the list is still open.

The date

The roadmap page says no dates, and it means it. This post makes one exception, once: I am aiming at 2027-01-01. A New Year gift.

A target is not a promise, and I would rather write the number down than hide behind “soon”. If the work is not ready, the date moves. What will not happen is a silent cut of scope to hit the date; if something has to leave 0.1.6, it leaves with a paragraph saying so.

The gift can be late. The box cannot be empty.

What does not change

L0 stays a frozen contract: adding or removing a primitive is a versioned change with downstream notice. The two implementations stay byte-identical on what they emit, the self-host compiler stays in the loop, and generated artifacts keep their own commits. Every claim still needs a criterion that could prove it wrong.

The stable channel is 0.1.5.1 until 0.1.6 ships. The Beta channel follows main, so the work described here is visible in the Playground while it is being built. There is still no open world, no reflection and no runtime type names.

Where to follow

#922 tracks 0.1.6, the roadmap carries the order, and the timeline carries dates once they are facts. I will keep posting the smallest program that shows a gap; if you hit a wall, that small program is the most useful thing to send.
