The Finals Of 2026

I am spending the last weeks of 2026 on a short list: 0.1.5.2 as the official release, the first test build of 0.1.5.3, a Playground that feels more like a tool than a demo, and another pass over lifetimes and GC timing. This is the long version of that list.

This is the plan I am working to. 0.1.5.1 is still the stable channel today. I have an order; I do not have a day.

Where 2026 landed

2026 carried the project from 0.1.4 to 0.1.5.1. The surface grammar now has three editions: Tur 2026.1 is frozen, Tur 2026.2 closed in November, and Tur 2026.3 is the one I am still moving. A file can pin the reading it was written for. That was the point of the whole exercise.

Domains became blocks you enter and leave: the domain starts when you walk in and ends when you walk out. All eighteen kinds ship, domain:for walks positions, and tree and graph edges became syntax (child_of, couple, mutex). LSPE v2 arrived in four parts: the questioner (E1–E8), the counter-asker (Q001–Q013), the LSPC pre-flight sheet, and a ledger with a proof hash per site and a .lspe sidecar.

Borrow lifetimes landed without a lifetime syntax. &T and &mut T are first-class in parameters, returns, struct fields and generic arguments; lifetime_auto is the default from tur2026.3 on, and the common cases need no annotation. The self-hosted compiler caught up on 2026-11-08: E041 now appears only when a region does not hold. lgc gave the collector a clock: lgc start(10ms) { … } opens a collection window with a cooperative budget, lgc end; pauses, and no_gc plus lgc is E039 because there is no collector to control.

The store reached 13 packages, 332 modules and 2791 public functions. I added ChaCha20, Poly1305, AEAD, AES-CTR/GCM, x25519 and ed25519, AF_UNIX with SCM_RIGHTS, a TLS 1.3 client core, an event loop, atomic file operations, a bounded channel, a job queue, a small HTTP server and a Windows declaration surface. The Playground put the reference compiler and the LVM in a browser tab; Java libraries became callable without a JVM; hot update became a release-shaped thing; wasm32 became a design record.

I keep the open list in the last section. I would rather show it than round it off.

The order for the last stretch

The order is short. 0.1.5.2 becomes the official release first. The beta line already carries the domains, LSPE v2, the lifetime dimension, the expanded store and the Playground snapshot. Promotion is the week where the channel, the artifacts, the signatures and the notes have to agree. I move the stable channel when both implementations read the same surface and the criteria are green.

After that, 0.1.5.3 opens with its first test build. I want the next line runnable before I call the year done. The test build is where the things that are still design get their first executable shape. I am keeping the order because a stable floor makes the next build easier to judge.

Maybe the most useful meaning of “official” is narrow: a tag, signed artifacts, a channel the Playground's Stable side can point at, and a note that says what is missing. Some open design items will still be open after it. The list stays in the last section until its criteria exist.

Three tracks for the same weeks

Playground

I am spending the next weeks on the Playground because it is the first place most people meet the compiler. I want a faster first load and a smaller snapshot; an editor that keeps line numbers, syntax, diagnostics and selections correct; clearer actions for Run, Check, Build, IR, AST, Potato and machine code; and a layout that works on a phone. Beta will keep following main; Stable will keep pointing at the released tag. No uploads, no server.

Lifetimes

I am widening lifetime_manual past the &a T marker, bringing borrows in generic arguments to both implementations, and making the diagnostics name the source, the line and the path. Maybe the zero-annotation cases stay the default; the explicit cases should stay rare and obvious.

GC timing

I am tightening the lgc budget semantics, measuring the collector on the four-tier ladder, and writing down what each tier promises under load. The budget gates starting a collection; it does not preempt one already running. no_gc stays a real mode, and no_gc plus lgc stays E039.

What “finals” means here

A school year ends with finals because the calendar says so. A language ends differently. I close an edition when the surface stops moving and the criteria are green. December is a good time to stop and look; it is a bad reason to declare the work finished.

Maybe the honest year-end statement is smaller than a launch: I can describe the project in one page without inventing anything. That is the bar I want for next year.

What remains open

The list is still here. lifetime_manual needs a wider annotation surface. Extension domains and the solid body of the graph model are still design. The wasm32 backend is a design record; I cannot use it as a backend yet. The store's platform and protocol edges are boundaries; the Windows modules wait for PE FFI. The five store gaps are still only a branch. I have no dates for any of them.

The previous test-build note is here; the Playground is here. Everything else is in the repository.

A year is a useful unit for looking back and a bad unit for declaring a language finished. — Loment Dev Fujo
